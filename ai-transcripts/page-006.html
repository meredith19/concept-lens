Semantic problems — one worth a decision

1. Granularity mismatch (the real one). "Order has shipped" is a claim about an order; "Left the fulfillment center" is a claim about a package. A multi-package order can have one package dispatched and two not, so the two propositions aren't strictly identical. By Concept Lens's own standard — a mapping asserts two facts state the same thing — a domain reviewer would likely challenge or scope this one.

Three ways out, and I'd take the third: - Reword the Returns fact to be package-scoped, which weakens the "user-facing label" you wanted - Accept it silently — worst option, since the tool exists to surface exactly this - Keep the label and make the rationale carry the caveat, e.g. scoping it to single-shipment orders. That's what rationale and reviewedBy are for, and a demo where the provenance text does real work is stronger than one where it's filler

2. Containment is cheap here. Shipped has exactly one modeled fact, so anything mapping to it automatically "includes all confirmed meaning of Shipped." The UI will announce "Returnable includes all confirmed meaning of Shipped, plus more" — a strong-sounding claim produced by a one-fact model. It's honest, and it demonstrates something true: derived containment is only as strong as the modeling granularity. Not a blocker, but expect it on screen.

3. Domain plausibility — asserting that Returnable entails having shipped is a policy claim rather than a definitional one. Returns owns the concept, so it's their call to model it that way. That's the ownership model working correctly, not a flaw.
